Aurora Silicon: what fits your M1 Air kernel

Goal. You asked which parts of aurora-silicon could go into your own kernel (T212, AsahiLinux tag asahi-7.2.15-1 plus kernel.config) on this MacBook Air M1 (J313, T8103, macOS 13.5 system firmware). Investigation only: nothing was changed and nothing was rebooted.

Short answer. Aurora's kernel is not a set of patches you can lift. It is a separate, very fast-moving fork on Linux 7.3 that has about 700k lines of Apple-specific changes against your tree, and most of its work targets M3, M4, M5 and the Neo. Five things are cheap and safe for your Air: (1) a true-peak limiter on the J313 speaker graph, (2) Wi-Fi country codes, (3) testing CPU boost, (4) the ambient light sensor plus the keyboard-backlight daemon, and (5) the camera denoiser. The big items (Touch ID, USB-C external display, Thunderbolt docks and faster GPU submission) all need the whole fork or a large port. Touch ID also writes to the Secure Enclave storage that macOS shares on your disk, so I would not try it on a dual-boot Air yet.

What Aurora Silicon is

  • Young and fast. The org was created on 2026-09-15 (measured, GitHub API). Its kernel repo has 599 open issues and merges dozens of PRs a day: its latest 40 commits span about two days. Much of the work is LLM-assisted, as the PRs disclose with Assisted-by: LLM (measured, commit messages).
  • Its relationship to Asahi is a hostile fork. It started from Asahi's kernel, m1n1 (as aura), u-boot, asahi-audio and the UCM config. Its policy says to never upstream to Asahi Linux or upstream Mesa, and Asahi names are being retired (measured: agent-guidelines/policy/ip-and-tainted-data.md line 52, agent-guidelines/README.md). So nothing will flow back into the Asahi tags that T212 tracks. Every piece you take is a patch you carry yourself.
  • It has no stable kernel line. integration/aurora is Linux 7.3.0. aurora-edge is 7.3 alpha, 465 commits behind integration. aurora-stable is 7.1.12 and was last touched on 2026-10-15, which is older than your 7.2.15. There are no release tags (measured: Makefile and GitHub compare).
  • Size of the gap. git diff asahi-7.2.15-1 integration/aurora gives +205k lines in drivers/gpu/drm/asahi, +94k in drm/apple, +82k in drivers/soc/apple and +105k in the Apple device trees (measured, shallow clones in /home/engstrand/.cache/checkouts/github.com/aurora-silicon/linux).

1. Speakers

Limiter on the J313 graph: do this. Aurora rendered every Asahi speaker graph offline. The J313 graph clips worst of all: 556 and 586 clip events in 30 s of music, peaking at +7.0 dBFS, and even −12 dBFS pink noise clipped 370 times. The amps take integer samples, so anything over full scale is hard-clipped before speakersafetyd can act. You hear it as crackle on bass hits (inferred: commit b30c50a in asahi-audio). The fix adds an LSP limiter_stereo (threshold 0.891, which is −1 dBFS) after woofer_lim in firs/j313/graph.json. That is 20 lines on top of asahi-audio v5.0, the version you ship (measured: git diff v5.0 HEAD -- firs/j313/). You already provide lsp-plugins to PipeWire. Risk: it only lowers peaks. Fit: a patches entry or a substitute* phase on asahi-audio in /home/engstrand/guix/modules/engstrand/asahi/sound.scm. The same diff also relabels the J313 mic as MONO instead of AUX0, which helps some apps.

speakersafetyd. You run 1.1.2 (measured, pgrep). Upstream 3.0.0 has no J313 changes, only blackbox removal, DynamicUser and a GPL relicence, and Aurora's fork adds only M4 Macs (measured: git log). There's nothing to gain.

speakerguardd v2 replays macOS's own protection unit using the Mac's tuning bundle. The M1 Air unit (atsp) is marked "later" and the project as a whole is "1.0.0-alpha, not for use yet" (measured: README). It also needs Aurora's kernel speaker interlock in macaudio.c. Do not use it now.

2. Touch ID (Secure Enclave)

It does target your Mac. The M1 profile was developed on a J313 running macOS 13.5 (22G74) firmware. That is your exact model and your asahi,os-fw-version of 13.5 (measured: tools/aurora-sep/M1-SUPPORT.md, /proc/device-tree/chosen). It enrolled, matched, and restored after a reboot. A real desktop unlock through PAM "has not been observed".

What it needs.

  • A new apple_sep driver: 64 files, +46k lines of Rust and C replacing Asahi's 375-line sep.rs. It also needs the spi2 and mesa fingerprint DT nodes, an SPI chip-select timing change and a new UAPI header. Its Kconfig depends on OF_DYNAMIC, TRUSTED_KEYS and the 7.3 Rust abstractions. A patch set on 7.2.15 would be a real port (inferred: Kconfig and file list).
  • Per-machine calibration apple/mesacal-j313.bin, carved from the iBootSystemContainer by aurora-touchid-setup.
  • libfprint with about 7 Aurora patches, fprintd with 2 or 3, a touchid-rearm PAM module, and systemd units written for Fedora, Arch and Nix. All of that would need porting to Shepherd.

Why the risk is high on your machine. You have macOS installed: a 103 GB APFS container and a 500 MB iBootSystemContainer (measured: lsblk).

  • The driver writes directly into the xART "gigalocker" .gl file inside the APFS iBootSystemContainer, which every OS on the Mac shares. These writes are now always on and have no off switch (aurora-silicon/linux#2591).
  • On M1 the SEP cold-boots, and creating Linux's keybag rewrites a session record that predates Linux. The guard is therefore off by default on T8103, and the docs say to treat macOS Touch ID as unverified until you check it there. This was seen on T6000, and "T8103 untested".
  • The enclave's anti-replay counters cannot be rolled back, so there is no backup or restore. Erasing that state is "for macOS's own tools, or a DFU restore" (inferred: nixos-aurora/sep/XART-SAFETY.md, aurora-xart README).

My call: not now (guess). If you ever try it, first image the 500 MB iBootSystemContainer read-only, use the whole Aurora kernel rather than a port, and check macOS Touch ID afterwards.

3. CPU

Boost. On your running kernel /sys/devices/system/cpu/cpufreq/boost is 0. The P-cluster stops at 2988 MHz and never uses its 3096, 3144 and 3204 MHz boost states (measured). Asahi lost boost-by-default upstream in commit ddef17bb. Aurora turned it back on in f677f7c87b, then turned it off again for the M2 (2ab25489ef) because the fanless M2 Air delivered no extra clock and only ran 4 °C hotter. The M1 is unmeasured. At most it is +7% on single-thread work (3204/2988). Fit: measure first (as root: echo 1 > /sys/devices/system/cpu/cpufreq/boost, then perf stat -e cycles on a pinned single-thread loop). If it helps, set it at boot with a Shepherd one-shot; you don't need a kernel patch. The Aurora patch does not apply to 7.2.15 as it stands (measured: git apply --check).

cpuidle. Aurora lowered CPU PD's target residency from 10 ms to 100 µs (9143929f0e) and raised its exit latency to 30 µs (74351910c8). On an M2 that lifted single-thread clock from about 2.6 to 3.1 GHz and cut idle P-cluster power from 0.55 to 0.30 W. Both patches apply cleanly to asahi-7.2.15-1 (measured). But your kernel uses the ladder governor, the only one built (CONFIG_CPU_IDLE_GOV_MENU is not set), and ladder promotes on exit latency, not residency. Your cores already spend most idles in CPU PD: cpu0 had 74.5M PD entries against 8.2M WFI, cpu4 6.7M against 0.9M (measured: sysfs, 16 h uptime). So the residency change does nothing here, and the exit-latency change would make ladder slightly less eager (inferred: drivers/cpuidle/governors/ladder.c lines 90 to 157). Low value. Skip it unless you also switch to menu or teo and measure.

Not for M1: the CLPC, PMP, MCC, cluster-pwrgate and sleep.c work targets the T8132, T8140, T8142, T6040 and T6050 (measured: compatible tables).

4. GPU

Aurora has G13/G14 (M1/M2) host latency paths: 6a6b3716d3, ce494c700e and 471887dd7b, which add inline publication, a dedicated event worker and a CPU capacity hint on syncobj waits. On an M1 Pro, per-submission latency fell 26–47% on light compute at depth 1–2 and 27–29% on light render. GPU-bound rows did not change and glmark2 stayed flat (inferred: commit text). None of the three applies to 7.2.15: asahi_core.rs is missing and gpu.rs and workqueue.rs have diverged (measured). This would be a real port into the Rust GPU driver, so wait for Asahi to do something similar, or take the whole kernel. Several robustness fixes, such as per-client queue limits and mapping-collision refusal, mostly target the Neo and M4.

5. Display, USB-C and Thunderbolt

  • External display over USB-C (DP alt mode). Aurora routes both J313 Type-C ports to dcpext and limits the DMA window (f6ac4a6200 and d4951cf2a8). The routing DT patch applies to 7.2.15, but the driver side (DCP fabric, typec-routes) exists only in Aurora's drm/apple (+94k lines). The J313 commit itself says "External display output … remain untested". Not portable; wait.
  • Thunderbolt PCIe tunnels (docks with Ethernet or USB controllers) were enumerated on T8103 and tested on the J293 MacBook Pro 13 M1 with a CalDigit TS3 Plus (57b3f3af33). This needs drivers/thunderbolt (+13k), pci/controller (+12k) and new DT. Not portable.
  • Backlight: nothing new for J313.

6. Sensors, keyboard light, camera, video

  • The ambient light sensor is dead on your machine. aop_als is loaded but /sys/bus/iio/devices is empty. The driver requests apple/aop-als-cal.bin, and your vendor firmware in /run/asahi-guix/firmware/apple holds only ISP files (measured). The AOP log shows the CT720 sensor itself is found. Asahi's installer collects this file from ioreg in macOS (asahi_firmware/als.py, added Dec 2025, after your install). Fit: boot macOS once and export CalibrationData from ioreg -r -a -n als -l, or re-run the Asahi installer's firmware step, then put the file in the vendorfw cpio. After that, kbd-light (MIT, libc only, reads kbd_backlight and in_illuminance_input) is a small Guix package plus a system service. It has an idle-dim mode that suits Hyprland.
  • camera-denoise (MIT, meson, needs libpipewire) adds a denoised camera node next to the apple-isp one. M1 has no board profile, so you get only the CPU temporal filter. That costs about 3 ms per 1080p frame on one core, less at the Air's 720p (inferred: README). Low risk, and it is opt-in per app.
  • Hardware video decode. Your kernel already has VIDEO_APPLE_AVD=m and apple_avd is loaded, but there is no /dev/video for it because apple/avd-fw-v2-t0.bin is missing (measured). The firmware is open source: AsahiLinux/avd-fw, MIT, built with clang. Add it plus Aurora's libva-v4l2_request avd-fixes branch for mpv and ffmpeg. Chrome and Firefox need more work: Aurora carries a Firefox RDD sandbox patch. No kernel change.

7. Small extras

  • Wi-Fi country. Your regulatory domain is GB and you are on DFS channel 100. Without brcm,ccode-map-trivial the BCM4378 firmware stays on worldwide XZ. Aurora's 7d9a9aedd7 names the J313 as tested, and it applies cleanly to 7.2.15 (measured). Fit: add the property in your existing dtbs-with-ane overlay step in /home/engstrand/guix/modules/engstrand/packages/omarchy-ane.scm, or as a kernel patch.
  • aurora-diag snapshot and check --expect would complement check-kernel.bb for the first boot of the T212 kernel. It is a development tool and not needed.
  • Battery (not Aurora). Yours has 830 cycles and holds 3.21 of 4.38 Ah (73%), and charge_control_end_threshold is 100 (measured). The running kernel already supports an 80% cap. That is the cheapest battery win available, and it needs no new code.

How it would fit T212

  • Kernel patches go in the patches list of %source in /home/engstrand/guix/modules/engstrand/asahi/kernel.scm. Only the Wi-Fi DT and the cpuidle pair apply as they are. Each one changes the .config-identical proof from T212, so take them after you have booted the T212 kernel once.
  • Config: none of the picks needs new options (APPLE_SEP, USB4_APPLE_SOC, VIDEO_APPLE_AVD and IIO_AOP_SENSOR_ALS are already m).
  • Userspace (the limiter, kbd-light, camera-denoise, avd-fw, libva) is ordinary Guix packages and services. The firmware files go through asahi-firmware.scm.
  • Switching the whole kernel to Aurora (a different %source) would bring Touch ID, DP alt mode, Thunderbolt and the GPU paths at once. But there is no stable branch or tag, it moves by hundreds of commits a week, and its features assume their own boot chain (aura copies ADT values into the DT). I wouldn't (guess).

Method

Shallow clones of integration/aurora, aurora-stable, aurora-wip and Asahi's asahi-7.2.15-1 in /home/engstrand/.cache/checkouts/github.com/aurora-silicon/linux. The small repos are in the same parent directory. Patch fit was tested with git apply --cached --check against a temporary index of the Asahi tag, with no checkout. Machine facts came from sysfs, /proc/config.gz, dmesg and lsblk on this Air. No builds were needed and nothing ran as root. Not measured: boost on M1, the limiter by ear, and Touch ID.
