Ghostty plus Rex with fewer parsers

T225, 2026-11-24. An investigation: nothing on main changed. Every claim is marked measured, inferred or guess.

Short answer.
  • Today each pane's output is parsed three times: by Rex's server, by rexg-render, and by Ghostty. measured
  • You cannot get to one while you use Rex. Rex's server always parses, because that is how it keeps your screen while no window is open. The floor is two. inferred
  • The one way to reach two without writing a renderer is option (a): give each Rex pane its own Ghostty window and copy the pane's bytes into it untouched. I built that as a 63-line spike and it works. measured
  • It is not faster in any way you would feel (3.7 ms against 5.4 ms per keystroke). measured What it buys is less code and a whole class of bugs gone (the cursor ones), plus pictures in panes.
  • It costs the one-window look: the tab bar, status line and overlays must move to Hyprland and keyleader. Ghostty's own splits cannot be driven from outside on Linux. measured
  • The macOS Rex app runs two parsers per pane: Rex's server, and a Ghostty terminal per pane that a Ghostty GPU renderer draws directly. That is option (b), with a renderer library Linux does not have. See section 5. inferred
  • My pick: option (a) with a window per pane tiled by Hyprland, in three stages, each useful alone and each with a stop point. If you want to keep the one-window look, stop after stage 1.

A parser here means code that reads escape codes and keeps a grid of cells. There are three, and two small scanners that are not parsers.

Why three is the awkward number

tmux and Herdr have two: the server parses and also paints the whole window, and the terminal draws it. inferred Rex's server does not paint. It sends each pane's raw bytes. So something on your side must turn many byte streams into one picture, and that something needs its own parser. That is rexg-render. Parser 3 is the least risky of the three: it only reads our own simple paint commands, so it cannot disagree with a program. The fragile part is parser 2 and the glue around it.

3. The options

(a) Ghostty is the only parser on your side

A tiny relay runs inside a Ghostty surface. It copies the pane's bytes to the screen and your keys back. No libghostty-vt, no compositor.

Spike. /home/engstrand/.local/state/bbh/workflows/20261124-180329-007-investigation/evidence/spike/rex-pane.bb , 63 lines of bb. It skips the snapshot without decoding it (the format is a list of length-prefixed records, 10 lines to walk), asks Rex to print the screen once as escape codes (terminal.format, 2 ms), then copies bytes. I ran it in a real Ghostty on the hidden test display against a throwaway Rex server: colours, scrollback and typing worked. measured

The echo numbers are the time from writing one letter into the client to that letter showing in the client's output, with bash in the pane. measured A screen frame at 60 Hz is 16.7 ms, so both are under one frame. The 1.7 ms difference is real but you will not feel it. Most of it is rexg-render's limit of one frame per 8 ms (line 676). inferred

What it removes. rexg-render (704 lines), the libghostty-vt and rexg-render Guix packages, the snapshot decode, the cursor hand-off (all of T223), copy mode, scroll and mouse routing in model.clj (Ghostty does these itself), most of screen.clj. Pictures in panes start to work, since Ghostty sees the real bytes. inferred

What it costs. Two problems showed up in the spike, both real:

  • Double answers. A program asks “what terminal are you?”. Rex answers, and now Ghostty answers too. I typed printf '\033[c' and bash got ^[[?62;22c^[[?62;22;52c: two replies, the second as junk on the prompt. measured The relay must drop Ghostty's replies from the input. That is a small filter on a known list of reply shapes, but it is one more thing that reads escape codes.
  • First paint can be wrong. My first screenshot had two lines out of place, because the pane was resized while Rex printed it. measured The fix is to resize, wait for Rex's resize message, then print. Simple, but it must be right.

And one design cost: where the layout lives.

  • Ghostty splits: no. On Linux, Ghostty 1.4.0 offers nine actions to other programs over D-Bus: new-window, new-tab, new-window-command, present-surface, reload-config and four others. None makes, moves or focuses a split. measured new_split and goto_split exist only as key bindings, so we would have to fake key presses, could not say which pane a new split shows, and could not read the layout back. inferred This is not about your habits; it is that Ghostty cannot be told.
  • A window per pane, tiled by Hyprland: yes. new-window-command opens a window that runs the relay, with no new Ghostty process. hyprctl can place, move and focus windows. Hyprland becomes the layout, and Rex only holds the programs. Your four startup tabs each hold one pane already. measured (config.edn)

The tab bar, status line, which-key, minibuffer, tiny fingers and the floating layers then need new homes: Hyprland workspaces or groups for tabs, keyleader and Cmd+Space (which already exist outside the window) for menus, a bar for agent attention. Tiny fingers cannot draw labels over a window it does not paint; it would become a picker over text from terminal.format. inferred

On the bugs. T223: gone, Ghostty draws the program's real cursor. T210: no decode at all, but no faster than today's fixed path. Reconnect: same, and each window now has its own stream to bring back, so the relay must stay a dumb pipe to one bb process that owns the connections. inferred

(b) Keep libghostty-vt per pane, but skip the outer parser

  • A non-parsed protocol into Ghostty does not exist. The only way to put cells in a Ghostty window is to write escape codes to it. inferred (no such action or option in ghostty +list-actions or D-Bus measured)
  • Hand our libghostty state to Ghostty to draw: not on Linux. The packaged library is libghostty-vt, the parser alone, with no renderer. measured (modules/engstrand/packages/ghostty.scm line 258)
  • Draw it ourselves: that is rexmux. /home/engstrand/guix/desktop/build/rexmux/src/gui.rs is a GPUI window that draws the cells with no outer terminal. Two parsers. It is about 3,300 lines of Rust and you left it in T207 to have no Rust and to get Ghostty's fonts. measured

What it removes: parser 3 only, the harmless one. What it keeps: parser 2 and all its glue, so T223-style bugs stay. Verdict: no.

(c) Rex's server state as the only source

Rex does not offer it. A data stream has one format, rex.data.terminal.v1: a snapshot, then raw bytes. measured (rex api describe data.connect) There is no stream of cells. The nearest thing is terminal.format, which returns the whole screen on request in about 2 ms. measured Polling it per pane per frame would work badly for scrollback, cursor shape and speed. inferred

One small piece is worth taking: read the text for tiny fingers and copy from terminal.format instead of from rexg-render. That removes text_reply and its request plumbing (about 40 lines of C and 15 of bb guess) and makes Rex the one source for “what does the pane say”. A full version of (c) needs Superlogical to add a cell stream; worth asking, not worth waiting for.

(d) Keep three, thin the middle

Accept the three parsers and cut what makes them hurt: land W293's cursor fix, take the small piece of (c), and nothing else. Removes almost no code. Keeps the one-window look and all 35 parity checks. This is the fallback if (a) fails its gates.

4. My pick and the plan

Option (a), a window per pane tiled by Hyprland. It is the only one that reaches two parsers without a renderer of your own, it deletes the most code, and it removes the hand-off that caused T223. It does not make anything faster, and it changes how the thing looks, so it goes in stages you can stop at.

Stage 1. Pop out a pane (about a day guess)

Turn the spike into a real ghostty-rex pane BLOCK, and an M-x command “Pane: pop out” that opens the focused pane in its own Ghostty window through new-window-command. Add the reply filter, the resize-then-print first paint, and reconnect. Nothing is removed; the current client keeps running beside it.

You get: pictures and Ghostty's own scrollback, selection and search for any pane you pop out, today.

Gate: a week of use with pi and Neovim in popped-out panes, no junk on the prompt, no misplaced first paint. If the reply filter cannot be made reliable, stop here and go to (d).

Stage 2. A second mode beside the first (several days guess)

A new MUX= choice next to ghostty-rex. One bb process holds the Rex connections; each pane is a Ghostty window; Hyprland tiles them; tabs become Hyprland workspaces or groups; menus stay with keyleader and Cmd+Space; agent attention goes to a bar or notification. make mux switches back in one command, as it does now.

Gate: the parts of the parity list that still apply pass (startup tabs, project switch within 300 ms, rename, split, reboot restore, bbh routing), and you prefer it after a week.

Stage 3. Delete

Only if stage 2 becomes your daily mode: remove rexg-render, screen.clj, the copy-mode, scroll and mouse code, and the two Guix packages. Until then both modes share rex.clj, names.clj, model's connection machine and the sidecar.

If you want to keep the one-window look, do stage 1 and (d), and stop. You keep three parsers, with a way out for any pane that needs pictures or misbehaves.

Risks

  • Reply filter misses a reply shape. Junk appears on a prompt. Limited to the popped-out pane; found fast. inferred
  • Ghostty and Rex disagree about a pane after a resize or a reconnect, because Ghostty's copy is rebuilt from a print-out, not a snapshot. Fix: reprint on resize and reconnect. Scrollback is printed too, pictures are not. inferred
  • Many windows. A project switch must repoint or reopen several windows. The spike's attach took about 120 to 150 ms for a 25 MB pane and a few ms for a normal one, but opening Ghostty windows was not timed. guess that it stays under your 300 ms budget; stage 2 must measure it.
  • Rex itself. The stream format and API moved from revision 14 to 18 in three days (T224's doc). The snapshot framing I skip is Ghostty's published format, so that part is stable. inferred
  • Work lost. Stage 3 retires code and checks written in T207 to T223. That is why it is last and optional.

5. How the macOS app does it

I took apart Rex.app from /home/engstrand/Downloads/Rex.dmg (Rex 0.1.0-preview.21, the same server version as yours). I unpacked it into its own folder and only read it: nothing in it was run. One correction first: rea ( https://github.com/morluto/rea ) is not Rex's source code. It is a reverse-engineering tool, so I used its method by hand: list the libraries, read the symbol and file names, read the strings. measured

In one line. The Mac app runs two parsers per pane, not three: Rex's server, and one Ghostty terminal per pane inside the app, which a Ghostty GPU renderer draws straight to the screen. There is no outer terminal re-reading escape codes. That is option (b), done with a renderer library Linux does not have. inferred

What is inside

  • No web view, no SwiftTerm, no xterm.js. The app is native Swift. It links AppKit, SwiftUI and Metal, and only one outside framework, Sparkle, for updates. measured llvm-objdump --dylibs-used, saved as app.dylibs in the evidence folder
  • Ghostty is built in. Three Ghostty modules are compiled into the app: GhosttyVT (libghostty-vt 1.0.1, the parser), GhosttyRender (a Metal renderer, with its own shader file and a Nerd Font) and GhosttyTerminal (an AppKit SurfaceView). The bundle id com.superlogical.swift-ghostty says it is Superlogical's copy of a Swift package for Ghostty. measured strings; Resources/swift-ghostty_GhosttyRender.bundle
  • The server is a separate Go program that uses go.mitchellh.com/libghostty, Mitchell Hashimoto's Go bindings for libghostty-vt. It links no graphics at all. measured

Next to each option

  • (a) a Ghostty window per pane. The Mac app does not do this. It keeps one window and draws every pane itself. So it neither proves nor rules out (a). What it does show is that Superlogical, with Ghostty's own people nearby, chose not to pipe bytes into a stock terminal. inferred
  • (b) libghostty-vt per pane, drawn ourselves. This is exactly the Mac app. It proves (b) works well, and gives one window, two parsers, pictures and no cursor hand-off. inferred But its renderer is Metal and AppKit, it is not published ( https://github.com/mitchellh/swift-ghostty and the ghostty-org copy both say “Repository not found” measured ), and Linux has no such library: your Guix package is the parser alone. On Linux, (b) still means writing or reviving a renderer, which is what rexmux was. inferred
  • (c) cells from Rex. The Mac app does not use it either. Even Superlogical's own client takes the snapshot and raw bytes. That makes it likely that a cell stream will not come soon. guess
  • (d) thin the middle. Today's rexg-render already does the Mac app's first half: a libghostty-vt terminal per pane, fed snapshot then bytes, replies dropped. The only thing it does differently is the end: it turns cells back into escape codes for Ghostty instead of drawing them. That last step is the whole third parser. inferred
Does this change my pick? No, not yet. The Mac app is the best way to do it, and it is option (b). But on Linux (b) needs a GPU renderer you would own, and you left rexmux to avoid that. Until a renderer exists that you do not have to write, (a) is still the cheapest way to two parsers, and (d) the way to keep one window. What changes is the long view: if Ghostty ever ships its renderer as a library on Linux (a GTK or OpenGL version of GhosttyRender), switch to (b) like the Mac app. rexg-render would keep its terminals and draw them itself, and the bar, tabs and one-window look would all stay. guess
