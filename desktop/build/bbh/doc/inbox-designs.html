What the evidence says

  • Noticing is slow. Median time to a reply is 16 min, and 6 of 21 replies took over 2 h. Nothing notifies you about a new ask; the push listener exists only while the inbox buffer does.
  • Under half of asks get answered in the inbox. Of 55 asks in 6 days, 21 were replied to there. The rest were answered by a Router steer, a cancel, or overtaken when the Worker finished.
  • You triage in batches. o820, o826 and T39 pasted several asks into the Router at once, or said "decide yourselves".
  • The current outline is cluttered: 83 top-level headings, 67 of them ARCHIVED, and landing boilerplate repeated on three done threads.

Shared fixes, whichever design you pick (cheap): one folded ARCHIVED heading; done threads collapse to one line without landing boilerplate; "since you last looked" from the existing per-client cursors (inbox.edn :cursors); a notification when an ask opens.

Mocks are drawn for the 210×59 tty frame, scaled down. Threads T72, T77, T80, T82, T84 and T85, their landings and the 67 archived threads come from the live bbh inbox org on 2026-11-08. T72 a53 and T82 a55 are real earlier asks replayed as open. T85 a56 stands in for the question this page leads to; its id and times are made up.

Build cost on top of T82

S–M, about 1–2 days. Reuses T82's pick!, answer!, send! and draft. New: a queue order (stopped first, then oldest), one renderer for a single ask, a send-then-advance wrapper. Undo needs a delayed outbox, which is M on its own.

Tradeoffs

  • Fastest flow when 3 or more asks wait.
  • Matches the batch-triage habit, including "decide yourself".
  • Works the same at 80 columns and on the phone.
  • Loses context across asks on the same thread.
  • Useless for overview; needs another view alongside.
  • Still relies on you opening it: no help with noticing.

Superhuman Mail

Hierarchy

  • Splits divide the inbox into areas of focus, so you process one kind of mail together and switch tasks less. The default split is Important / Other: Important collects mail from people, Other the automated mail. You add your own, such as VIP, Calendar, Team, or one per tool (Figma, Jira, GitHub), from a sender, label, star or any search query[2][3]. Tab and Shift Tab move to the next and previous split[1].
  • Conversations in a split, one per row. J and K move between them, Enter opens one, N and P move between its messages[1].
  • A one-line summary sits above every conversation and updates as new mail arrives; i shows more. Superhuman calls it its first AI feature you don't have to remember to use[9].
  • Folders outside the inbox, each one G then a letter: Done (G E), Reminders (G H), Starred, Sent, Drafts, Muted[1].

Life of a conversation

Key flows

Triage one at a time

Decide what to do with each email the first time you open it: clear it, defer it, or make it a to-do. Each action takes the message out of the inbox [4]. E marks it done and moves straight to the next message without dropping back to the list [4]. Vohra: "when I archive the same email, I immediately see the next one. I don't have to make any decisions at all. This creates flow." [5]

Defer

H sets a timed reminder; a reminder can fire only if the recipient does not reply, or regardless [3]. The method warns against overuse: snoozed twice means it needs focused work, so make it a to-do instead [4].

Clear a backlog

⌘A Select All From Here, then E [2][1]. Get Me To Zero archives older mail at once and leaves unread and starred mail in place [4].

Reach the goal

Inbox Zero is the concrete, achievable, rewarding goal. Reaching it shows striking imagery, and onboarding wipes the slate clean for users far from it [5].

Keyboard first

  • Superhuman Command, ⌘K, does any action and also shows its shortcut for next time [6]. It exists because users knew what they wanted to do but forgot the key; it is the "make the next action obvious" condition of flow [5].
  • Palette rules: the same key opens and closes it everywhere and returns focus to where you were; when the key is taken in a context (⌘K makes a link in the editor), pressing it twice opens the palette [6].
  • Training: a 30-minute one-to-one onboarding call teaches the shortcuts, and advanced users get a harder goal: Inbox Zero without touching the mouse [5]. More than 50 shortcuts are on one PDF [12][1].
  • Core keys: E done, ⇧E not done, H remind me, Z undo, J/K next and previous conversation, Enter open, R reply, ⌘↵ send, ⌘⇧↵ send and mark done [1].

Attention

  • Splits are the priority order: when you have a few minutes, open the VIP split first. Phone notifications can be limited to VIPs, so other mail never interrupts [2].
  • Read Statuses show when and on which device each recipient opened what you sent. The post says they start off and are turned on through ⌘K, and that you can block tracking pixels sent to you [10].
  • The one-line summary lets you skip reading: "sometimes we don't even read the email" [9].

Answering

  • R reply or Enter reply all, then ⌘⇧↵ sends and marks the conversation done in one key, which advances to the next [1].
  • Instant Intro: "Say thank you, move to BCC, and start responding. All in an instant." Snippets insert saved phrases or whole emails [11].
  • Under two minutes, reply now and archive; otherwise remind or make a to-do [3][4].

Speed principles

  • The 100 ms rule: every interaction should be faster than 100 ms, the threshold where it feels instantaneous (after Paul Buchheit) [7].
  • Measure the share of events under 100 ms rather than a 90th percentile, because it says how often the app is fast for the user [8].
  • Flow needs three things: make the next action obvious, give clear feedback at once without distraction, and balance challenge with skill [5].
  • Controls must be rapid and robust, so that a mistyped key cannot do damage [5].

plannotator

Two surfaces. A review tab opens in the browser when an agent finishes a plan, when you ask for a code review, or when you annotate a document; you decide there and the tab closes [14]. The Inbox is one local window per machine where agents leave messages, questions, files and guided reviews while they keep working; nothing holds a session open while you answer [13][15]. T59 compared it with bbh; the Comparison below already maps its Board to design 2 and its per-thread view to design 4.

How plans and diffs arrive

Inline annotation

  • Selecting text opens a toolbar: Deletion, Comment, Quick label (presets such as "Needs tests", "Out of scope"), Looks good, and a Global comment not tied to text [19].
  • A replacement or insertion is asked for in a comment, and the agent applies it when it revises [19].
  • Annotate with --gate gives three exits, returned as approved, annotated or dismissed; approval notes are guidance, not a request for another revision [20].

Inbox hierarchy

  • Sidebar: Search (⌘K, the Inbox's only shortcut), Starred, Drafts, the Board when turned on, and one entry per project, the repository an agent wrote from [16].
  • List: one row per thread, in sections: Stopped on you, Holding up work, Waiting on you, New since you looked, Sent, Quiet (folded). Sent waits on the agent, so it sits below New. A row shows project, agent, subject and how many questions wait [16].
  • Thread: reads like email; questions render as cards; files and guided reviews open beside it [16].
  • Board (off by default): work that outlasts a message, in lanes Backlog, Todo, In progress, In review, Done; a mid-work question leaves the card in place marked Waiting on you [18].

Life of a question

Attention

  • The list does not reorder under you. New agent activity waits behind "N new in <project>" until you act, and nothing you do in an open thread reorders it; it catches up when you leave the thread [16].
  • Browser notifications for every new thread and every new question, except in the thread on screen; plain messages in a thread you have don't notify [16].
  • A soft doorbell when a question arrives and no Inbox tab is in front, once per burst: more questions within 30 seconds stay quiet [16].
  • Agents set the priority themselves: a question block can say Stopped: (the agent cannot go on) or Holds up: (work that waits); an :::action block is a task only you can do, with Done or Can't do it [15].

Answering

  • Click an option to pick, type words, annotate an attached file, then Send (Mod+Enter). One Send answers every message of that session [16].
  • The line under your reply says where it is: "Queued · <agent> is working; it gets this when its turn ends" with Send now, "Sending to <agent>…", then delivered. For an MCP-only agent: "Saved for <agent>" [16].
  • 👍 Looks good answers a plain message in one click and moves the thread to Quiet until the agent writes again [16].
  • When the session that asked has ended, the row says Session ended and the thread offers Send to another session or Archive [16].
  • Undo with Z or the toast, for a few seconds, before Mark read or 👍 reaches the agent; bulk Mark all as read and Archive all per project are undoable the same way [16].

Status and refresh

  • plannotator inbox status exits 0 when running, 1 when not, 2 when the process holds the port but does not answer; --json for scripts [17].
  • After restart, agents waiting for replies reconnect within a few seconds and a reply that waited arrives once; the open tab needs a reload [17].
  • The Inbox starts by itself, in the background, when an agent calls it [15].

Recommendation

Build 5 + 1 on top of 3, after the shared fixes.

The data says the problem is noticing (median 16 min, 6 of 21 over 2 h, nothing notifies) and batch triage (o820, o826, T39 pasted into the Router). Design 5 fixes noticing cheaply. Design 1 is the batch-triage flow, including "decide yourself". Design 3 is the cheapest real overview, because it is one query over events that already exist. Designs 2 and 4 look best at 210 columns, but they cost the most and break at 80 columns and on the phone.
